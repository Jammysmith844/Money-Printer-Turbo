How each calculator works

1. Savings goal

Answers: "How long until I reach my target?" It compounds your starting balance monthly at the rate you enter, adds your monthly deposit, and tells you when the balance crosses the target. If the target is never reached within the period you enter, the monthly result tells you how much longer it would take at the same deposit.

2. Compound interest

Answers: "What will my investment be worth in X years?" It uses the standard compound interest formula with monthly compounding:

FV = P × (1 + r/12)n + PMT × [ ((1 + r/12)n − 1) ÷ (r/12) ]

Where P is your starting amount, PMT is your monthly deposit, r is the annual rate, and n is the number of months.

3. Dividend income

Answers: "How much passive income could this portfolio pay?" Year 1 income is simply investment × yield. If you enter a dividend growth rate, the calculator projects each subsequent year's payout growing at that rate, and sums the total across the period.

Worked examples

UK saver: £20,000 house deposit

Starting from £0, depositing £400/month at 4.5% AER. You reach £20,000 in about 46 months (3 years 10 months), having deposited £18,400 and earned roughly £1,600 in interest.

US investor: 30-year retirement pot

$500/month for 30 years at 7% annual return. Total contributed: $180,000. Final value: roughly $610,000. The compounding accounts for about $430,000 of the final amount.

Canadian dividend portfolio: $25,000 in bank stocks

$25,000 at a 4.3% yield produces $1,075 in year 1 — about $90 per month. With 3% annual dividend growth, the payout reaches roughly $1,445 by year 10, and total dividends collected over the decade come to about $12,300.

Good to know

  • Rates change. The figures in the reference table reflect typical 2025 rates. Always confirm with your bank or broker before acting.
  • Inflation matters. A 4.5% savings rate with 3% inflation gives a real return of about 1.5%. The calculator shows nominal values only.
  • Tax is not included. UK Personal Savings Allowance, US federal tax on interest, and Canadian dividend tax credits all affect your net return. Talk to a tax professional.
  • Dividends are not guaranteed. Companies can cut or suspend payouts during downturns. The growth rate you enter is an assumption, not a promise.
  • Compound interest cuts both ways. Investing early matters more than investing large. A 25-year-old saving £200/month usually ends up with more than a 45-year-old saving £500/month.
  • Use real numbers for your situation. The defaults are starting points, not recommendations.

Frequently asked questions

Are the interest rates in the table current?

They reflect typical rates from 2025. Savings rates move with central bank policy — the Bank of England, Federal Reserve, and Bank of Canada all adjust their base rates periodically. Always check your bank's current published rate before committing.

Does the calculator include tax?

No. All figures are pre-tax. UK taxpayers can earn up to £1,000 in savings interest tax-free (Personal Savings Allowance for basic-rate payers), and Cash ISAs are fully tax-free. US and Canadian rules differ. For accurate net figures, speak to a tax adviser.

Why does compound interest take so long to show results?

Compounding is exponential, so the early years feel slow and the later years feel fast. A 30-year investment typically earns more in the last five years than in the first fifteen. This is why starting early matters so much.

Is a 7% return realistic?

Historically, a globally diversified equity index has returned roughly 6–8% per year before inflation, over long periods. In any single year, returns can be much higher or much lower. Past performance does not guarantee future results, and investing carries risk of loss.

What is the difference between AER and APY?

They are the same concept with different names. AER (Annual Equivalent Rate) is used in the UK; APY (Annual Percentage Yield) is used in the US. Both express the effective annual rate including compounding.

Does the dividend calculator account for dividend cuts?

No. It assumes dividends grow steadily. In reality, companies can and do reduce or suspend dividends during recessions or sector downturns. Treat the projection as a best-case illustration, not a forecast.

Does this site store my inputs?

No. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, no cookies are set, and closing the tab clears everything.

Limitations

  • The calculators produce estimates, not guarantees. Actual returns will differ.
  • No tax, fees, or platform charges are included. Fund management fees of 0.2–1.0% per year can meaningfully reduce long-term outcomes.
  • Inflation is not modelled. A £100,000 pot in 20 years will not buy what £100,000 buys today.
  • The compound interest calculator assumes a constant rate every year. Real markets fluctuate.
  • Dividend projections assume consistent yield and steady growth — neither is guaranteed.
  • The savings goal calculator does not account for rate changes over the savings period.
  • This tool does not recommend specific products, banks, or brokers. For personal financial advice, consult a regulated adviser.
